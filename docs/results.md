# Results (2026-09-09; revised after independent review)

**Question (registered, doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/HQ4A8):** is Crohn's disease (CD) heritability distributed differently across
postnatal gut cell lineages in East Asian (EAS) and European (EUR) ancestry?

**Answer: no evidence of a difference, with limited precision.** The registered primary test (joint Wald test of EAS minus
EUR differences in tau* across 8 lineages) gave W = 4.04, df = 8, p = 0.853 (Table T3). Per the registered
interpretation table this is "no evidence of an ancestry difference", **not** evidence that the distributions are equal:
the study could not exclude ancestry differences as large as the lineage enrichments themselves (see Interpretation).

## How the run was done
- Single run of the registered analysis: script `code/13_primary_analysis.py`, committed to version control **before** it was run;
  results committed immediately after. No rerun of the primary analysis.
- Data, LD references and model as registered (plan sections 2–3), including `--overlap-annot`.
- **Implementation error found by the independent review (corrected post hoc, T8):** Step A used the atlas field
  `Sample name` as the donor. Two children contributed separately sorted cell fractions under several sample names
  (T036, T036NEG, T036POS; T110NEG, T110POS), so the reference has **15 donors (7 adults, 8 children), not 18**, and
  those children were over-weighted in the gene-specificity averages. The registered plan specified donors as the unit.
  The reviewer's in-memory recomputation found the corrected top-10% gene sets overlap the ones used at Jaccard
  0.85–0.95 per lineage (36–122 genes differ per lineage); K = 8 is unchanged. Steps A–E were
  rerun with true donors (code/20, 21, 24); the result (T8) is reported **alongside** the registered result, which
  remains the registered primary result.
- All tables below are generated by `code/19_report_tables.py` from the files named in each heading.

## Interpretation (registered section 5)
- **Primary p >= 0.05: no evidence of an ancestry difference.** No single lineage differed after Holm correction
  (all p Holm = 1.00; T2).
- **What the data can and cannot rule out.** Marginal 95% CIs of D span -0.40 to +0.27 tau* units across lineages;
  simultaneous (Bonferroni, 8 lineages) 95% CIs span -0.50 to +0.36 (T2). The T-cell CI (simultaneous -0.50 to +0.21)
  includes a difference as large as the whole European T-cell tau* (+0.35). So **the study could not exclude ancestry
  differences as large as the lineage enrichments themselves.** The registered minimum detectable difference from the
  pipeline-validation simulations was > 0.72 tau* (80% power, one-lineage difference); the real data were more precise than the
  simulations (real SE of D = 0.50–0.79 of simulated; post hoc), and scaling suggests the detectable difference is
  greater than about 0.5 (approximate, post hoc). Either way it exceeds every observed lineage tau*.
- **Validation.** Validation (i) found no evidence that the two European datasets differ (p = 0.47). This is not
  positive evidence of agreement: FinnGen estimates are about twice as imprecise, and some lineages diverge
  (mesenchymal FinnGen +0.19 vs de Lange -0.03, z = -1.83; plasma cells de Lange +0.18 vs FinnGen -0.01). Validation
  (ii) found no evidence of an EAS-FinnGen difference (p = 0.44). The EAS side rests on one dataset (no independent EAS
  cohort exists; accepted limitation).
- **Robustness of the test.** None of the registered sensitivity analyses gave evidence of a difference (T4;
  p = 0.52 to 0.995). **But the per-lineage estimates move (T4b):** dropping every SNP with an ImmunoChip contribution
  (about 10% of regression SNPs, concentrated in known immune loci) lowers T-cell tau* in both ancestries (EAS +0.20 to
  -0.03; EUR +0.35 to +0.14) while myeloid stays similar (EAS +0.27, EUR +0.21). Much of the T-cell signal therefore
  sits in those immune loci. The estimated T-cell difference is similar in both versions (-0.15 vs -0.17).
- **Descriptive estimates (not registered tests).** In the registered model, the largest tau* values in all three CD
  datasets are for T cells and myeloid cells (T2). This ranking depends on the analysis choice: without ImmunoChip-
  covered SNPs the EAS T-cell tau* is about zero, and with top-5% gene sets the EAS order is T cells > plasma cells >
  myeloid (T4b). Negative tau* (e.g., neuronal) means less heritability near those genes than near expressed genes in
  general (the control annotation); the cause is not established here.
- **Exploratory (T5–T7), labelled exploratory.** Excluding the NOD2 region, using ulcerative colitis, using small- or
  large-intestine-only references, or using bootstrap gene sets each gave no evidence of an EAS-EUR difference. Across 51
  fine cell types, EAS and EUR tau* correlate (Pearson r = 0.73); the fine-type gene sets overlap, so no p-value is
  given. The highest fine types are T-cell and NK types in both ancestries, and no fine type differs by more than
  |z| = 1.79. EAS tau* tends to be lower than EUR tau* for T-cell types (e.g., T cells D = -0.15, 95% CI -0.40 to
  +0.10); this is not significant and the T-cell estimates are correlated, so it is not a finding.

## Post hoc follow-ups after independent review (2026-09-09), labelled post hoc
- **Donor-corrected rerun (T8).** With 15 true donors: primary W = 2.16, p = 0.976; validation (i) p = 0.477,
  validation (ii) p = 0.233. Per-lineage tau* change by at most 0.044 in EAS and de Lange and by up to 0.10 in FinnGen (B cells). Same conclusion.
- **Aligned-block check of the registered covariance assumption (T9).** On 950,331 SNPs common to all three datasets
  (so LDSC's jackknife blocks cover the same genome stretches), the EAS and EUR block-level errors are positively
  correlated (0.24 to 0.40 across lineages), as the reviewer predicted, so the registered summed-covariance test is
  conservative. Computing Cov(D) directly from aligned blocks gives W = 7.22, p = 0.513 (summed formula on the same SNPs:
  W = 4.39, p = 0.820) and standard errors of D 13–22% smaller. **Still no evidence of a difference.** The narrower
  intervals still include zero for every lineage; the simultaneous 95% intervals span -0.46 to +0.32, so differences
  as large as the T-cell enrichment are still not excluded (T cells: D = -0.18, simultaneous -0.46 to +0.09; the
  marginal interval -0.38 to +0.02 is unadjusted and post hoc and is not a finding).
- **IIBDGC readme archived** (data/raw/gwas/IIBDGC_IBD_EAS_sumstats_readme.sst.txt, SHA-256 recorded): it states
  the Direction cohort order SHA1, ICH1, KOR1, JPN1 used for per-variant effective N.

## Deviations and clarifications (logged in DECISIONS.md, including entries added 2026-09-09 after the review)
1. Step A used sample names as donors (implementation error; corrected rerun reported alongside, T8).
2. Step C adds a P column computed from Z because this LDSC version's munge step requires one (format only).
3. Under the registered "munge defaults", munge removed 72,526 EAS SNPs (6.8%) with effective N < 9,669 (variants
   missing several cohorts); LDSC then removed SNPs with chi2 > 80 (93 EAS, 384 de Lange, 0 FinnGen; T1).
4. The simulations and the exploratory analyses fitted models without `--overlap-annot` (verified in the simulations
   to leave coefficients and jackknife values unchanged; full-data tau read from LDSC's log, ~5 significant digits).
   The primary analysis and the registered sensitivity analyses used `--overlap-annot` as registered.
5. Sensitivity (d) used +/-50 kb windows for both the lineage and the control annotations.
6. Exploratory 1: the registered text says "the 65 fine cell types"; that count came from feasibility-stage metadata without Step
   A's cell filters. Applying the registered rule (>= 100 cells, >= 3 donors) after Step A's filters gives 51 (the
   donor count used the same sample-name error as Step A).
7. Exploratory 3 (UC): new downloads on 2026-09-09 (code/15; EAS UC MD5-verified). EAS UC variants were matched to 1000G
   through the identifier table built from the CD file (same cohorts and positions). UC cohort sizes from Liu 2023
   Methods (SHA1 2,400; KOR1 1,569; JPN1 1,769; ICH1 1,124 UC cases; same controls as CD). Step C gained optional
   cohort-size and effective-N arguments; defaults (used for CD) are unchanged.
8. Exploratory 4: the plan did not define the large intestine; atlas regions LargeInt + REC were used (appendix
   excluded). The neuronal lineage fell below the lineage threshold in both regions (K = 7).
9. Exploratory models used the primary control annotation (all expressed genes, main atlas); about 8% of fine-type
   genes lie outside that control's gene universe. Bootstrap gene sets (E5) lost up to 79 genes when matched to
   coordinates (1,414–1,489 genes kept).

## Limitations
- No independent East Asian validation cohort (accepted before registration).
- **Precision:** the study cannot exclude ancestry differences as large as the lineage enrichments (detectable
  difference greater than about 0.5 tau*, post hoc approximation; registered simulation estimate > 0.72).
- **Covariance assumption of the registered test:** Cov(D) = Cov(EAS) + Cov(EUR) treats the two ancestries' estimation
  errors as independent. LDSC's block jackknife also captures variation from where causal variants lie in the genome;
  if ancestries share causal variants, their errors are positively correlated and the registered test is conservative
  (W was below K in all 22 analysis variants; they reuse the same data, so this is suggestive, not a test). The post
  hoc aligned-block check confirmed positively correlated errors (T9); the conclusion is unchanged. The
  simulations drew causal variants independently per ancestry and could not detect this.
- Step A donor error (above; corrected rerun gives the same conclusion). The registered donor-bootstrap stability
  figures treated split samples as separate donors; with true donors they are similar (median Jaccard 0.68–0.87).
- The EAS meta-analysis includes an ImmunoChip cohort; the primary analysis handles it through per-variant effective N.
  The test conclusion is unchanged without those SNPs, but T-cell tau* is not (T4b).
- LDSC intercepts: de Lange 1.07 (residual inflation not due to polygenic signal) and EAS 0.997 with LDSC noting
  "Ratio < 0 (usually indicates GC correction)". Liu 2023's text does not mention genomic-control correction and
  reports per-cohort LDSC intercepts of 1.02–1.06; the meta-analysis file's intercept of 0.997 is consistent with a
  correction having been applied somewhere, but this is not established. LDSC
  estimates the intercept freely, but cohort-level genomic control could interact with ImmunoChip coverage.
- Cell-type reference from 15 healthy donors; neuronal lineage is small (376 cells) and its gene set is the least stable.
- Heritability here is SNP-based and common-variant; rare variants (e.g., NOD2 coding variants absent in EAS) are not
  directly represented.
